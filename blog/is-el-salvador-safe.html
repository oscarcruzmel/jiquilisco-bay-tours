EL SALVADOR TRAVEL SAFETY · UPDATED 2026

Is El Salvador Safe to Visit in 2026?

El Salvador's security situation has changed dramatically. Here is what current official data and travel guidance say—and what visitors should still keep in mind.

Quick answer: El Salvador is currently under a Level 1 — Exercise Normal Precautions U.S. State Department travel advisory. Salvadoran authorities reported a homicide rate of 1.3 per 100,000 people in 2025, among the lowest reported rates in the Western Hemisphere.

How safe is El Salvador today?

For many travelers who remember El Salvador's reputation from years ago, today's numbers may be surprising. The country has experienced a dramatic reduction in homicides and gang violence.

Salvadoran security authorities reported 82 homicides in 2025, equivalent to 1.3 homicides per 100,000 people, down from 1.9 per 100,000 in 2024. The International Monetary Fund described the country's security improvement as a “dramatic turnaround” and, using 2024 data, called El Salvador the safest country in the region by homicide rate.

Salvadoran authorities describe the country as the safest in the Western Hemisphere. Because cross-country crime statistics can use different definitions and reporting methods, travelers should treat that phrase as an official Salvadoran claim rather than a universal ranking. What is clear is that the country's reported homicide rate has fallen sharply.

What does the U.S. government say?

As of June 25, 2026, the U.S. Department of State lists El Salvador at Level 1: Exercise Normal Precautions, its lowest travel-advisory level. The advisory says gang activity has decreased dramatically since 2022, contributing to a major decline in violent crime and murders.

The advisory still recommends ordinary precautions. It notes that robbery, car theft, sexual assault and other crimes can occur, and it advises travelers to remain aware of their surroundings, avoid displaying valuables, use established transportation options and minimize intercity travel at night.

What changed?

El Salvador was once known for one of the world's highest homicide rates. The government responded to gang violence with expanded policing, military involvement and a state of exception that began in 2022. These policies have coincided with an extraordinary decline in reported homicides and gang activity.

There is another side travelers should understand. The state of exception remains in effect and suspends some normal criminal-procedure protections. The U.S. State Department notes that tens of thousands of people have been detained and that some U.S. citizens have also been detained. Human-rights organizations have raised concerns about due process and detention conditions. Visitors remain subject to Salvadoran law.

Is El Salvador safe for tourists?

Current security indicators and the Level 1 U.S. advisory make El Salvador very different from the destination many travelers remember from a decade ago. Tourists are visiting beaches, volcanoes, historic towns, San Salvador and destinations such as Jiquilisco Bay throughout the country.

“Safer” does not mean risk-free. Travelers should use the same common-sense habits they would use in other international destinations.

Simple safety tips for visitors

  • Stay aware of your surroundings and avoid displaying expensive jewelry or large amounts of cash.
  • Use major roads and avoid unnecessary intercity driving at night.
  • Use reputable taxis, rideshare services or pre-arranged transportation.
  • Keep passports and valuables secure rather than leaving them in vehicles.
  • Use extra caution around Pacific beaches because currents and undertows can be strong.
  • For remote nature and water activities, use experienced local operators and understand that emergency response can take longer than in major cities.
  • Check the latest official travel guidance shortly before your trip.

What about Jiquilisco Bay?

Jiquilisco Bay is a large coastal and mangrove environment in Usulután. Visiting with local people who know the waterways can make planning much easier. Jiquilisco Bay Tours operates from Puerto El Triunfo with a local team and private experiences on the bay.

Because parts of the bay are remote, water conditions, weather and distance from major medical facilities deserve attention. Guests should follow their captain's instructions, wear provided life jackets during boat travel and communicate any medical or mobility concerns before departure.

So, should safety stop you from visiting El Salvador?

The current picture is substantially different from El Salvador's past reputation. Official Salvadoran figures show historically low homicide levels, and the U.S. government currently recommends normal precautions. Travelers should still prepare responsibly, follow local laws and pay attention to ordinary crime, road and ocean risks.

Bottom line: El Salvador has undergone one of the most dramatic reported improvements in public security in the Americas. For visitors, the best approach is neither fear nor complacency—check current guidance, use normal travel precautions and enjoy the country responsibly.
