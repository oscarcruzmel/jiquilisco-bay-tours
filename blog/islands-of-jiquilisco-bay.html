Islands of Jiquilisco Bay

Inside the bay is a shifting world of islands, islets, mangrove edges, beaches and waterways best understood from the water.

Jiquilisco Bay's island landscape is one of the reasons a boat is such a natural way to explore it. El Salvador Travel describes the broader ecotourism area as containing 27 islands and islets, while the Ministry of Tourism has identified seven principal islands: Cumichín, Espíritu Santo, Tortuga San Dionisio, El Recodo, San Sebastián, Sumaria and Arco.

Isla Espíritu Santo

Among the islands, Espíritu Santo receives special attention from the national tourism authority. It is known for abundant coconut palms and opportunities to appreciate birds, mangroves and tropical scenery.

A landscape shaped by water

The islands are part of a much larger wetland system. Ramsar describes Jiquilisco as a complex of estuaries, canals, sand bars, beaches, islands, freshwater lagoons and seasonally saturated forests connected to mangroves. That diversity means one part of the bay can look very different from another.

What you may see from a boat

Depending on route, tide and weather, a trip can include narrow mangrove waterways, broad open water, island shorelines, sandbanks, fishing activity, coastal birds and views toward the volcanic landscape of eastern El Salvador.

Responsible island visits

Not every shoreline should be treated as a recreation beach. Sensitive nesting and mangrove areas deserve space. Follow local guidance, avoid disturbing wildlife, carry out your waste and do not damage mangrove vegetation.

Did you know? El Salvador Travel describes the Jiquilisco ecotourism landscape as spanning roughly 55 kilometers of coastline, from the Lempa River mouth toward La Chepona.
