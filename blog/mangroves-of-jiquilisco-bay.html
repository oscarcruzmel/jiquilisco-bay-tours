Mangroves of Jiquilisco Bay

The mangrove forest is not background scenery—it is one of the systems that makes the bay function.

El Salvador's largest mangrove landscape

El Salvador's national tourism authority identifies Jiquilisco Bay as the country's largest mangrove area. Ramsar describes it as the nation's largest extension of brackish water and saltwater forest, with estuaries, channels, islands and connected wetlands.

Why mangroves matter

Mangrove roots slow water and trap sediment. The forest helps stabilize shorelines and reduce erosion while creating sheltered habitat used by fish, crustaceans, birds and other wildlife. Ramsar specifically recognizes the system's role in flood protection and soil stabilization.

A nursery connected to local livelihoods

The ecological value of mangroves is tied to the human economy of the bay. Fishing, shellfish collection and aquaculture have long been important activities in the surrounding landscape. Healthy wetlands support the broader food web on which many coastal species and communities depend.

What a mangrove trip feels like

Moving from open bay into a mangrove channel changes the experience quickly. Waterways narrow, the forest closes in and the trip becomes quieter. Depending on tide and location, visitors may see exposed root systems, birds and signs of fishing activity.

Visit without damaging what you came to see

Do not cut vegetation, remove wildlife, leave trash or enter sensitive areas without guidance. Boat speed and wake should be appropriate for narrow channels and nearby shorelines. A good mangrove experience is about observation rather than disturbance.

Scale: El Salvador Travel reports 22,912 hectares of mangroves within the broader Jiquilisco ecotourism landscape.
