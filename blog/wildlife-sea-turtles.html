Wildlife & Sea Turtles of Jiquilisco Bay

Mangroves, beaches and estuaries create habitat for an exceptional concentration of coastal life.

A nationally important bird habitat

The Convention on Wetlands describes the Jiquilisco Bay Complex as habitat for a large majority of El Salvador's coastal waterbirds and identifies nesting species including black skimmers, least terns, Wilson's plovers and American oystercatchers. El Salvador Travel likewise highlights the bay for its seabird population.

Four sea turtle groups documented around the site

Ramsar documentation identifies surrounding beaches as nesting habitat for green, hawksbill, olive ridley and leatherback sea turtles. Their presence is one reason responsible coastal tourism and protection of nesting areas matter.

More than turtles and birds

The Ramsar information sheet also documents threatened fauna associated with the wider ecosystem, including American crocodiles and caimans. Wildlife sightings are never guaranteed, and animals should always be observed from a respectful distance.

How visitors can help

Do not touch or feed wildlife. Keep noise low near bird concentrations, never drive through marked turtle nesting areas, avoid leaving plastic or fishing line behind, and follow instructions from local conservation personnel. If a turtle release or conservation activity is offered, use a legitimate organized program rather than handling animals independently.

Important: a wildlife tour is an opportunity to observe a living ecosystem—not a promise that a particular species will appear on demand.

Explore with respect.

A mangrove and boat experience is one of the best ways to appreciate the bay's habitats without treating wildlife as an attraction to chase.
